# Ubbe's Saga — specification (Beta 0.20.1)

How the game works **today**. Numbers are in `docs/NUMBERS.md` (generated). The long-term vision for Town Hall 6–10 is in `docs/DESIGN.md`.

## 1. The game in one paragraph
A cozy-but-harsh Viking village builder for the browser. Year 865, the village of **Kaldstrand** on a Norwegian bay. The player is Ubbe's line: raise the Town Hall from level 1 to 5 (10 in the full game), feed and house the people, keep morale and the gods content, build ships, raid and trade across the North Sea, and live through a realistic succession of jarls. Isometric, low-poly, muted colours ("Névoa do Norte", in the spirit of Bad North).

## 2. Modes
- **Relaxed**, **Challenge** (harsher events and consumption), **Sandbox** (endless stores: costs are free and every store is kept at 999, the HUD shows ∞; every building, limit and upgrade of Town Hall 5 is open from the start; construction, upgrades and the Town Hall finish at once, and the Town Hall needs no people or house level; all land is unlocked; fast speeds and test tools. People still arrive, work, eat and live as in the other modes; no raids on Kaldstrand).
- **Test**: choose Town Hall 1–5 and start with a prebuilt village (every building at its limit and highest level, full population, warriors, ships, herd), with test tools and 8×/16× speed. **Gallery**: every building at levels 1/3/5 and every house colour scheme side by side.
- Three save slots per device (`localStorage`, keys `ubbesaga.b14.*`; older saves do not load), plus Export/Import in the Saga panel.

## 3. Time
- 1 year = 45 days: 30 summer + 15 winter. 1 day ≈ 27 s at 1×. Speeds: pause, ½×, 1×, 2×, 4× (8×/16× in Test/Sandbox).
- Winter: more firewood, seasonal buildings rest, fishing drops, sickness rises (warm clothes halve it). Woodcutters keep full output.

## 4. Map
- 132 × 132 tiles, isometric (tile 64 × 32). Village centre `HOME = (63, 60)`. The map is fixed (same for every saga): **the fjord** (Beta 0.12).
- Kaldstrand sits at the head of a narrow fjord that winds south-east to the open sea; cliffs flank the outer fjord, a range closes the north, a river comes down from it and enters the fjord by the village, a second river feeds the outer fjord. A rocky iron hill rises just north-west of the village, a ridge runs south-west, islands sit at the fjord mouth and a lake lies in the western woods.
- Terrain: sea, beach, grass, forest, berry bushes, river, foothills, iron outcrops, mountains. Mountains are one low-poly heightfield (gentle foot, short cliff band, ridged grey peaks, snow only on the highest).
- **Starting land guarantee:** the generator makes sure the free starting land has iron (at least 10 iron tiles within 14 of the village; about 14 today), berries and forest near the village, and water within a few tiles for fishers; the village core is kept an open meadow.
- Raiders land only on sea joined to the map edge (never on the lake).
- Land is bought in 6 × 6 zones with **fame**; the saga starts with 21 zones (a 5 × 5 block minus its four corners, 30 × 30 tiles); the corners and everything further are bought; cost grows with distance (`30·d − 10`).
- Edges fade into sea-mist; unbought land is slightly darker with a faint tile grid, and each 6 × 6 block is outlined (brighter for blocks next to owned land).
- **Resource sources belong to one building and run out** (claims since 0.15.4, depletion again since 0.18.0): trees, berry bushes, iron outcrop tiles and fishing water stay on the map. Every woodcutter, berry gatherer, mine and fisher's hut claims the nearest free sources within its radius (6, 5, 4, 3 tiles), oldest building first, up to what it needs: **20 trees, 6 bushes, 5 iron outcrops, 5 water tiles** for full output; with fewer, output falls in proportion. Each source holds a yearly amount that is used up: **tree 50 wood** (woodcutters make 5 wood per worker a day), **bush 45 berries**, **sea tile 100 fish, river tile 70**, **iron outcrop 80 iron**. Used-up trees are drawn as stumps and grow back **3 days after winter ends**; bushes and water refill when summer starts; iron refills **every two years, when winter starts** (even years). A building whose own sources are all used up stops taking workers until they come back. The building sheet shows own sources, output %, how much is left and when it comes back; the tile sheet shows what is left on that tile.
- Trees and bushes go for good only with the **Remove** tool or when a building is placed over them (the game asks first); each gives a quarter of a season's output (2 wood a tree, 3 berries a bush).

## 5. People
- Each person has a name, age, gender, traits (~1 in 4), health, a job or a warrior role, and parents. Children (under 14) do not work; people over 50 work at half rate.
- The **jarl** leads; succession follows the heir (chosen among children). Lineage is meant to be realistic: about 5 jarls by Town Hall 10.
- **Morale** (0–100) from food variety, housing, firewood, feasts, the patron god, levy, events. ≥ 70: +10% work and more births; < 40: −15%; < 20 for long: revolt risk. The village glows gold / blue-grey / red with morale.
- The saga starts with **16 people** (Ubbe's household of 3, four couples one with a child, four young adults) **4 houses** and the **Jarl's House** (already built) next to the Town Hall (24 beds). The Town Hall stands in the middle of its cobbled square; it can be moved and rotated like any building (not demolished), and rotating it turns the hall and moves the plaza fire to another corner.
- **Attractiveness** draws newcomers when there is room and food; during the first year they come 1.6× faster. **Morale** changes how many come: above 55 ×1.5, at 70 or more ×2.2 (and more come as couples), below 40 ×0.5. From Town Hall 3, in the last 10 days of summer and in winter, newcomers wait while the wood would not cover 5 days of firewood.
- Thralls (from raids) work; soft or harsh policy.

## 6. Economy
- Buildings have worker slots; people are assigned automatically by **priority**, set per building: Stopped · Low · High (default) · Urgent. Urgent places are filled to the last post first (oldest first); then High places share the rest one worker at a time, then Low places. Berry gatherers stop in winter by themselves and go back to their own priority in summer. Every building sheet shows how many work there (e.g. "3 of 4 working here"); the hover tip and the People → Workplaces table show it too. Recruits for training may be taken from anyone not on an Urgent job. (Balance bots still set head counts themselves with `S.manualStaff`.)
- Output per worker × building level bonus: level 2–3 ×1.2 (fields ×1.3 at level 3), level 4 ×1.4, level 5 ×1.55. Non-field buildings get +1 worker slot at level 3.
- **Construction takes time:** a new building (and each upgrade) takes ½ day (1×1 decor), 1 day (up to 2×2), 2 days (3×2, 3×3) or 3 days (4×3 and larger, the Town Hall) and needs 1–3 builders, oldest site first. Builders are the **Town Hall's builder posts**: 2 + Town Hall level (3 at TH1, +1 per level), with their own priority on the Town Hall sheet; they only take people while something is being built. The plot shows scaffolding and a progress bar; with no builders the site waits. Ragnar's Ship-Tomb is raised by its own builders as before.
- **Limits:** houses, granaries and warehouses have no limit; the 1st and 2nd granary or warehouse cost the base price, the 3rd–5th each +30% on the previous one, then +50% each. Other buildings keep per-level limits. Decor unchanged.
- Storage caps: food (granaries), goods (warehouses), weapons (arsenal). There is no water resource: the village lives by the river.
- **Firewood:** each house burns 0.35 wood a day in summer and 0.84 in winter (−30% since 0.15.3) (houses at level 3+ ×0.75; the Town Hall burns as two houses). When logs run out, fires burn planks (1 plank = 2 wood), never below the plank reserve.
- Fisher's Hut: fishes the water within 3 tiles (5 tiles of its own for full output); less in winter (more at sea than on the river).
- Food sources: berries, fishers, the **Pig Pen** (pigs: meat and hides) and, from TH3, **Grain Field → Water Mill** (on the river; flour never spoils) **→ Bakery** (bread all year). The **Fodder Field** feeds pigs and sheep in winter (not people). Preserver (dried fish, jam) and Smokehouse (smoked fish and meat) keep food. The **Sheep Pen** gives wool (sails, warm clothes).
- Chains: wood → planks; iron → tools / weapons / armour; fish, berries → dried fish, jam; fish, meat → smoked; grain → flour → bread; grain → ale; honey → mead; wool / flax → cloth → clothes.
- **Ale and mead are not meals.** The Brewery and Beehives open at TH3; ale and mead are drunk at banquets and in the **Tavern** (opens with a Brewery), which uses a little each day and raises morale while it has a keeper and drink (more with mead).
- Wool, hides and odds-and-ends only appear once something can use them (or trade opens).
- **Reserves:** every good a workshop takes from the stores (wood, planks, iron, fish, berries, meat, grain, flour, honey, wool, flax, hides, ale, mead) can have a reserve, in steps of 10 up to 1,000. No workshop (carpenter, smithy, preserver, smokehouse, mill, bakery, brewery, weaver, tailor, tavern) uses goods below it; a workshop held back says "Holding: … is at the reserve". Eating, firewood (logs; planks only down to the plank reserve) and the player's own building and upgrades still use reserved goods. "Reserve for next Town Hall" raises the reserves once to the next hall's wood, planks and iron cost; "Clear reserves" sets them all to 0.
- **Production log:** every day the game records what each building (and herds, eating, firewood, spoilage, events) made and used of every good; it keeps a running average for summer days and one for winter days.

## 7. Town Hall progression
Requirements per level: **population + materials + at least one house at the highest level the current Town Hall allows** (houses can reach the Town Hall's level). No fame, building or saga-goal requirements; saga goals remain the early unlock path and give fame (for land). Each level unlocks buildings and raises limits.
- TH2: 110 wood, 70 planks, 20 iron, 22 people, a level-1 house · TH3: 190 wood, 150 planks, 50 iron, 10 tools, 32 people, a level-2 house · TH4: 320 wood, 270 planks, 100 iron, 20 tools, 100 silver, 48 people, a level-3 house · TH5: 460 wood, 400 planks, 170 iron, 30 tools, 250 silver, 20 gold, 80 people, a level-4 house.
- Town Hall 1 goals, in order: Settle the shore (berry gatherer + woodcutter → unlocks Carpenter, Preserver, Funeral Pyre) · Timber and planks (40 planks → Granary, Warehouse, Iron Mine, Smithy) · Ready for winter (preserver, granary, warehouse, 35 preserved food → Market; the Jarl's House is already built at the start) · Iron and tools (mine, smithy, 12 tools) · The first winter · A gathering place · A growing village · Town Hall 2. Older saves get any unlocks that moved to earlier goals. At level 3, if no shore is free for a shipyard, the jarl claims a coastal zone for free.

## 8. War, ships, raids
- Troops: skirmisher, spearman, warrior, archer, viking (TH4), berserk (TH5), mercenaries (hired, not trained). Trained at the training ground; gear from the smithy. **No warrior limit:** training works like assigning workers: the Training Ground sheet shows the free workers and a "+" trains a batch (up to 5, or 8 at level 2), limited by free workers and gear. Weaker troops at home can retrain as a stronger kind (they need new gear).
- Ships: karvi, knarr (cargo), snekkja (TH4, fast). War-ship cap rises with the Town Hall. **Berths:** each Harbour moors 2 ships (one each side of the pier), the Shipyard's own small pier 1. A new ship goes to a Harbour with a free berth, otherwise to the Shipyard pier; when all berths are taken the Shipyard cannot lay a keel. Ships at sea keep their berth. Harbours: 1 (TH3–4), 2 (TH5). The Harbour, Shipyard and World map sheets show berths used / free. Each has its own look: karvi with red-and-white shields and a striped sail; knarr short and broad with cargo under hides and a plain sail; snekkja long, low and dark with a dragon head and black-and-gold shields.
- **Harbour** (2 × 6): a long pier that must run from dry shore (its land end all on land) straight out to sea (its sea end all on water). A plank platform hugs the shore with a small boathouse (crossed dragon-head gable boards), a crane, nets and barrels; a lantern stands at the pier head. Ships at home moor alongside the pier, one each side (knarrs first).
- **Shipyard** (3 × 3): one row of dry shore for the boathouse (3 × 1, a road can run right beside it), a middle row with the quay where the ship is built, and a far row in the sea with the slipway pier; the whole plot, wooden platform included, is the footprint. The game picks the side that faces the sea. Old shipyards (4 × 3) move to the nearest spot that fits when a save loads.
- Raids: choose target, ships, crew, tactic, prisoners, whether the jarl sails. Animated departure and return. Loot, fame, prisoners, grudges; targets recover after plunder. Tribute from cowed villages.
- Provisions: crew × 0.75 meals × (2 × travel + 3 days). Ships only sail if the village keeps 3 days of meals at home, or the whole winter's meals when winter is 10 days away or less (never more than 40% of the food stores). Cowed villages pay half a full plunder as tribute. Provisions not eaten come home as dried fish.
- Regions: Scandinavia (TH3), Northern Isles (TH4), England (TH5 + 40 gold map).
- Defence (TH5): palisade with gates (drawn like roads), watchtowers, levy.
- **Raids on Kaldstrand (TH5+, not in Sandbox):** about every 40–75 days (summer only; 55+ in Relaxed) scouts sight sails and give 5 days' warning (modal, To do, War → Defence). Raiders (6 + 2 per year since TH5, ×0.75 Relaxed, ×1.25 Challenge, max 24) land on the shore outside the palisade, beach their ships and head for the Town Hall along the cheapest path; palisade segments cost more the stronger they are, so they hack through the palisade only when there is no cheaper gap (an unwalled shore is a gap). At most 3 raiders' blows count on one segment. Warriors at home (not archers) come out from the Town Hall, stay inside the ring and strike raiders who reach them or who hack at the palisade (spears reach over the stakes); archers in manned towers shoot within 7 tiles. Raiders who reach the Town Hall steal goods (6 silver or 3 of a good per grab, up to 20 each) and carry them back to their ships; slain raiders drop what they carry. Fallen defenders are wounded (Relaxed) or 45% die (other modes; never the Jarl). After the fight a report shows raiders slain, goods taken, dead, wounded and broken segments; victory gives fame and +6 morale for 20 days, a plunder −6. The game slows to 1× and the camera moves to the landing; a save mid-fight is settled without the scene on the next day (defence vs attack, a closed palisade adds 60–100% to defence; surviving raiders carry off up to 20 goods each). Raiders carry black-and-yellow quartered shields; ours are red with a pale boss.
- **Palisade levels and repair:** Stakes 150 HP per segment, Double stakes 300 (3 planks a segment), Iron-bound stakes 500 (3 planks + 1 iron a segment); gates ×1.3. Upgrades apply to the whole palisade (War → Defence). Damaged stakes are patched for free (¼ of a segment a day); broken segments leave rubble and a gap until repaired (4 wood, + planks at higher levels, per segment).

## 9. Trade
**Trade map** (button in the Market and on the Trading House, TH3+): the world chart with the partner villages (relation bar and number of requests on each marker, knarrs drawn on their routes). Each village has a **relation** 0–100 (starts 30: Cold < 20, Wary < 45, Friendly < 70, Sworn friends); it rises with deals the village likes (+1…+6, answered request +8, ×1.5 for a Trader Ubbe), falls with refused offers (−1, −3 for an insulting one) and drops to 5 while the village is angry after a raid. Relation also moves Market prices (sell +0.25% and buy −0.2% per point above 30). **Offers:** send N of one of our goods for silver or for one of its goods. The village values ours at base price (×1.35 if it wants it) and its own at base price ×1.05 if it sells it cheaply, else ×1.4 (iron and tools ×1.35 more). It accepts when ours ÷ theirs ≥ 1.12 − 0.4 × (relation − 30)/100 (−0.08 Trader, −0.06 Athelstan, −0.04 wise jarl; never below 0.8); within 70% of that it counters with a smaller amount; else it refuses, and hears no new offer that day. **Requests:** up to 2 per village, 18–30 days each, for a good it wants, paying 1.5× in silver or one of its goods. Accepted deals need a **knarr** in port: our goods leave at once, the knarr sails (cargo up to 250, silver and gold weigh nothing) and comes home after 2 × the voyage with the payment (goods beyond the store room are left on the quay).
**Market panel** (Market button in the bottom bar once a Trading House exists, also from the Trading House sheet; needs a harbour): one tab per partner village that trades with you; every good has Sell and Buy at that village's own price, in amounts of 1, 10 or 50 (arrows in bundles of 10), at any time of year. Selling lowers that village's price for the good, buying raises it; prices drift back about 10% of the gap a day. A sell price is never above 85% of the cheapest buy price anywhere. Buying stops when the stores are full. Partners unlock gradually (Skiringssal TH3, Hedeby TH3 + ship, Birka TH4, Dublin TH5, London TH5 + map). Each partner wants and sells specific goods; selling a lot lowers the price (recovers slowly); iron and tools cost ×1.35 to buy, and buying them raises their price (2.5% per unit, up to ×2.5, easing 1% a day); special orders from a merchant in port (summer, every 12 days, 4 days) pay +50%.

## 10. Ubbe's trait, the patron god, the Feast, advisors
- **New saga (opening):** after the difficulty, three illustrated story pages (The year 865, Ubbe, Kaldstrand) over the new village, then three pages of picture cards: **Ubbe's trait**, the **patron god** and the **first advisor** (one click on a card chooses and moves on; Back returns). Traits (bonuses only, kept for the whole line): Builder (construction and upgrades 25% faster, 10% cheaper), Warrior (+15% strength on raids and at home, training 25% faster), Trader (prices 15% better, relations rise 1.5× faster, deals accepted at 8% less), Provider (+10% from fishers, gatherers, fields, hives and pens), Skald (+15% fame, +4 morale). Older sagas: no trait, and the god with the most favour becomes the patron.
- **Patron god** (Odin, Thor, Freyja or Njord; Loki is no patron): the only god with favour (starts 50, falls about 0.35 a day). Gift at 50 (Odin +10% fame, Thor +10% battle strength, Freyja +10% farms/gatherers/pens and more births, Njord +10% fishing and fewer storms); blessing at 80 at the Altar (costs 30, 20 with a Hof; 30-day wait). Offerings from the Town Hall or Altar (Altar +50%): Odin 8 preserved food (dried fish, jam, smoked), Thor 6 iron, Freyja 15 berries, Njord 15 fish → +8. Events give favour only when they name the patron. Morale: patron ≥ 50 +5, < 25 −5. The Hof's gothi honours the patron.
- **Patron's wrath:** favour below 25 (not in the first year) brings a warning, and after 10 days the wrath (then 90 days' rest): Odin a plague (a fifth of the people sick; die in Challenge), Thor lightning (40% of wood and planks burn), Freyja blight (farms and gatherers −40%, no births for 20 days), Njord storms (fishers −60% for 20 days).
- **Feast** (Town Hall sheet, all levels; replaces banquets and festivals): plain (meals = 0.8 × people: +8 morale 15 days, patron +8, fame +4); with ale or mead from TH3 (0.6 × people meals + 0.5 ale or 0.35 mead per person: +12 / +14 morale for 20 days, patron +10 / +14, fame +8 / +14; mead also makes the next raid fight 15% harder). Each feast in the same season costs 50% more. Holy days (start of summer, start of winter, day 8 of winter) are announced; a feast within 5 days doubles the patron's favour and adds +3 morale. Fire, pig on a spit and dancers at the plaza while it lasts.
- **Advisors:** council seats 1 (TH1–2), 2 (TH3), 3 (TH4), 4 (TH5); six advisors to choose from.

## 11. Interface
- **PC (≥ 900 px wide):** top resource bar grouped (people · food · materials · wealth: silver from the start, gold once there is any); each item shows a detailed icon, its name and the amount (in the title font) side by side and the daily change, with a hover breakdown; the To do list warns "Only 1 bed left" / "Every bed is taken" in step with the People counter; date and saga goal under it; speed control and **To do** list on the right; a bottom **dock** (gold border all round, labels in the title font) with panels (Village, People, War, World, Saga, **Production**), build categories and Roads/Remove/**Upgrades**/Palisade; the **build tray** shows building pictures; building and panel sheets dock on the left; buildings light up on hover. **Clicking picks by tile:** the tile really under the cursor (elevation counted) is found first; the building on it is chosen, else the tree / bush / ore on it, and only an empty tile falls back to a building whose picture covers the point (tall roofs). Hover shows the same choice (natural tiles get a gold outline and their name).
- **Production panel** (dock; on phone from Village → Stores): one table of goods grouped Food (all food in meals, then each kind) · Materials · Farms and herds · Crafts · Weapons, with store, made a day, used a day, net and **Winter**: how many winter days the store lasts (green when it covers the rest of winter / a whole 15-day winter). The winter figure uses the winter average in winter; in summer it scales this summer's output (fields, berry gatherers, beehives and herds 0, fishers ×0.4 at sea / ×0.25 on the river) and adds today's eating, winter firewood (×1.3 if the Völva foretold a hard winter), fodder and spoilage. Each row has its reserve (− / +). Clicking a row shows its **production chain** (raw goods on the left, buildings as pictures with what one worker makes, how many you have, arrows with amounts, "or" between alternative recipes), then "Made by" / "Used by" for the season and, in summer, the expected winter day. The panel is wider (660 px) and refreshes each day.
- **Upgrades list** (dock button with a green count of upgrades you can pay for): a floating panel on the right with the Town Hall and every building that can rise a level (same type and level grouped, e.g. "House ×4"), picture, level → level, cost with missing goods in red and "Needs … more"; ready ones first. Clicking moves the camera to the building and opens its sheet (again for the next one of a group).
- **Phone:** same top bar (swipe), bottom navigation, Roads/Build buttons, bottom sheets; build menu with pictures; To do list collapsed.
- **Guide** (new sagas only): an illustrated card with 11 steps (seasons, food and wood with sources that run out, builders, worker priority, fish, planks, food for winter, firewood with this winter's wood need, Production panel and reserves, the patron god and the Feast, tools and the Town Hall). Each step has a picture strip (building pictures or small drawings), the days until winter, an optional task list that ticks itself, a button that starts the placement or opens the right panel, a back arrow, and the dock button it talks about pulses gold. Steps with tasks move on by themselves when done. On PC it sits on the left under the date bar and moves right of a docked sheet. It can be closed and reopened from the Saga panel.
- **Event choices:** each choice shows the action, then a red "You pay" line and a green "You get" line with signed amounts and resource icons (also for festivals and the jarl's funeral).
- **Remove tool** (next to Roads): removes buildings (half the materials back, all while still under construction), roads, trees and berry bushes.
- **Build categories:** Homes · Food · Farms · Materials (wood, planks, iron) · Crafts (smithy, weaver, tailor) · Storage · Community (market, pyre, altar, Hof, tomb) · Military (training ground, arsenal, watchtower) · Sea · Decor. Build cards show what the building does in one line and what it needs (water, trees, ore, tools); a locked card says what unlocks it in a highlighted line ("Unlocks with: Town Hall 3", "Unlocks with: saga goal “Ready for winter”", or both); every card ends with its cost as large icons + amounts (goods you lack in red); the building sheet starts with that line, the full description and a "Needs" line (workers, location, tools, road); production buildings show an "Output from workers" bar: output is proportional to the workers present (2 of 4 = 50%), and each worker adds 100 ÷ slots %.
- **Icons:** colour pictograms for the dock panels, build categories, roads and palisade; build cards and upgrade lines show costs as icon + amount (name on hover).
- **To do list:** Town Hall ready, food/firewood shortages, firewood for the coming winter (last 10 days of summer), full beds, idle people, buildings in trouble, the current saga goal, next Town Hall requirement, ships ready to sail. Each item moves the camera or opens the right sheet / placement.
- **Roads and palisade:** click start, click end (the plan is then fixed), Build or Cancel.
- **Placing and turning buildings:** Rotate (R) turns a building to any of 4 sides: the first two turns are its two pictures (mirror images) with the door on a visible face (lower-left / lower-right); the next two use the same pictures with the entrance on the far side (upper-left / upper-right), so the walls you see have no door, and the door tile, path and road connection are behind. The button's arrow shows where the door will be. Buildings whose picture cannot turn (Water Mill, Watchtower, Shipyard) have no Rotate button; the Town Hall square keeps 4 turns. The gold "door" marker while placing, the path to the road and the night light sit exactly at the drawn door (table `DOOR_AT` in `09_state.js`, checked by `tools/ui/doorcheck.py`).
- **Music:** pieces with recorded instruments (VSCO-2 CE, public domain): 4 summer pieces (~10 min: harp, flute, fiddle, hand-drum grooves) and 4 winter pieces (~10.5 min: low strings, horn, frame drum, wind), shuffled within the season with a short breath between them; when the season turns the piece fades into one of the new season. Special pieces: the fleet sails, the ships come home, a feast, a new Town Hall level, the death of a jarl; then the playlist goes on. Music starts at the first click or key (browser rule); note button under Home toggles it; Saga panel has on/off, volume and the piece now playing. Files live in `music/*.mp3` (source `source/music/`, composer in `tools/music/`), fetched when first played and kept offline by the service worker.
- **World map:** full screen, large chart (Iceland to the Baltic, Norway to Frankia); drag/swipe to move, wheel/pinch to zoom; mist at the edges and over lands not yet open, their places still visible (England only lightly misted, so the goal is always in sight). Opens framing Kaldstrand and every open place. Norway and Sweden are labelled; Birka sits on an island off the Swedish coast.
- Text: "God" / "Gods" are always capitalised in game text (José, 0.20.1).
- Fonts: Marcellus SC for everything (titles, numbers and all text, also on the map). Classic look: dark wood bars, parchment sheets with dark headers, white building cards whose pictures are cropped and centred automatically. PC keeps the central bottom dock (panels, build categories, roads).

## 12. Art direction
- **Palisade:** a closed wall of upright split pine logs packed shoulder to shoulder, sharpened tops, in a low earth bank; level 2 adds an inner binding rail and taller logs, level 3 iron bands; gates have two heavy log posts each side, a plank walkway and a red pennant; snow on the tips in winter.
**Style: "Frost & Ember"** (chosen Beta 0.12; inspiration Frostpunk, Valheim, Banished): low-poly, flat-shaded, a warm golden summer and a cold blue winter, cast shadows, a slow time-of-day cycle and warm lights in the dark. The style board is the artifact "Ubbe's Saga — 6 estilos de arte".
- **Time of day** (`24_zlight.js`): one light cycle lasts 3 game days. Summer: short night, short dawn, a long warm day, short dusk; winter: shorter day, longer night. The whole scene is graded (soft-light colour, a multiply tint at night, a coloured haze at the top, a sun glow, a vignette). Night stays readable (blue, not black).
- **Shadows:** buildings, trees and the palisade cast soft shadows that swing with the sun (long at dawn and dusk, short at noon). Each building's shadow follows its own shape (walls, gable or hip roof, towers, sheds); the light trodden ground under a building hugs it instead of filling the square plot (fenced and paved yards keep the square).
- **Lights:** from dusk to dawn each building throws a warm pool of light by its door (its own windows and door glow in the drawing), fires and the Town Hall more strongly.
 **Buildings (Beta 0.16.0):** every building is drawn from one style kit (`source/src/20_zzstyle.js`; every choice and the reasons in `docs/art/STYLE.md`): fine lines, a visible door with frame, straps and stone step on every building, stone footings, visible timber frames, small glowing lattice windows with coloured shutters, crossed barge boards at both gables, no flags. Roofs are straw thatch, blue slate, black tarred scale shingles, brick-red boards or turf. Houses are gable cottages that grow each level (window at L2, shield and woodshed at L3, second window at L4, carved barge ends at L5) in 8 colour schemes (random per house, chosen in the house sheet). Jarl's House: longhouse with black shingles on a cut-stone plinth and a carved ridge-beam with dragon heads. Town Hall: red-board great hall on a dark plinth with an Urnes-portal vestibule, a raised lantern from TH3 and a cleared plaza round a feast hearth. Hof: black stave church with galleries, a tower on the ridge and a sacred pole. The others (Granary stabbur, Warehouse, Woodcutter, Carpenter, Smithy, Market with booths and a well, Iron Mine adit, Berry hut, Fisher's Hut on stilts, Preserver, Smokehouse A-frame, Pig and Sheep Pens, Training Ground, Altar with five god-posts, Funeral Pyre stone ship, wattle-fenced Fields with growing crops, stone Water Mill, Bakery, Brewery, Weaver, Tailor, Tavern, Watchtower, Arsenal, Harbour, Shipyard, Thrall Pen, Ship-Tomb, Trading House, Beehives, decor) follow STYLE.md. The Funeral Pyre burns only for a day and a half after a cremation. Round and shaped buildings keep grass on the rest of their plot. **Grass:** tall grass in three tones that sways in a slow wind, straw seed heads and sparse summer wildflowers, over a smooth colour drift across tiles; fine blades and flowers only when zoomed in. Roads are "trodden earth" (ruts, puddles, stones, grass tufts over the edge). Winter turns everything to snow.

## 13. Technical
- One HTML file built from `source/src` modules; canvas 2D renderer; no libraries. Installable PWA (manifest + service worker, offline after first load). Music is the exception to "one file": `music/*.mp3` (about 18 MB), cached in its own service-worker cache that survives updates.
- Render cost is dominated by trees and tile fills; tile colours, mountain triangles and fog are cached. Buildings without animation are kept as cached images per zoom (`20_zzstyle.js`, off with `window.__NOCACHE=1`) and redrawn when they change. Keep an eye on `tools/balance/perf.py` (ms per frame at three zooms) after visual changes.
